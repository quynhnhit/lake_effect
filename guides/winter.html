What do I actually need in the winter?

Published November 10, 2026
By Quynh-Nhi TranMy credentials: winter is my favorite season, I chase the winter sun, I’ve survived 7 Chicago winters, and I’m always cold.

Whenever I talk to my family about living in Chicago, they are always shocked when I tell them that I love winter. There is really nothing that compares to winter morning sun, and I highly recommend watching a sunrise over the lake when you have a chance.

That being said, to prepare for Chicago winters, you will want to have one of each of the essentials. I am a strong proponent of shopping your own closet, so don’t feel the need to buy anything unless you don’t already have it.

Casual winterwear

Gloves

I would argue this might be the most important purchase. My dad has always reprimanded me for putting my hands in my pockets, so I always have a pair of gloves on me in the winter. In general, I would pick fleece-lined leather gloves. Leather is hardy against super cold wind and water resistant, so if they get wet, your hands won’t freeze off.

I wouldn’t try too hard to find a pair with touchscreen pads; they don’t work very well. Mittens are also a very good option because your fingers can keep each other warm!

Hats

The next most important thing on my list is something to cover your head and ears. If your head is cold, you will feel cold. If you don’t want to buy anything, you should be fine in a hoodie, but as someone who is always cold, I really appreciate having a beanie or balaclava on. I have knit my go-tos, but I have also collected quite a few.

Scarves

My mom would say this is the most important one. Not only can you wrap a scarf around your neck and face in the cold, you can wrap it around your head like a balaclava or set it on your lap like a blanket.

Scarves are worth the investment: the nicer wool and alpaca ones tend to be less itchy, warmer and antimicrobial. These are a little pricier, but you really only need one, and they make great holiday wishlist items.

Base layers

The secret to getting through winter is learning how to layer. It’s an art, but you’ll get the hang of it. I like having both cotton and wool layers on hand: cotton above 40°F, wool below 30°F, and the middle 10 degrees depend on what’s clean :). Thin leggings or long johns are nice below 20°F as an extra barrier against the wind.

For this category in particular, don’t worry about buying anything new. As long as you have a long-sleeve top and pants to layer under your clothes, you’re all set.

Everything else

Boots

My hot take is that you don’t really need to buy new shoes for the winter, especially in Evanston. The sidewalks and pathways get plowed and salted very quickly (normally before 8 a.m.!), so I don’t often feel the need to wear my snow boots. I normally wear a pair of shoes I don’t mind getting dirty.

If you are in the market, I would just get one pair (and pack a cute pair of shoes in your bag). You don’t need true snow boots, but a practical pair is always nice to have. Personally, I commute in weatherproofed, rubber-soled leather boots and break out my Sorels when I want to play in the snow.

Brands I’d look at:SorelBlundstoneUGG snow boots

Coats

This will likely be the most expensive purchase! I have collected coats over the years as things have gone on sale. Anything that is puffy and water resistant should be good enough for this winter. Ideally the stuffing would be goose down, which is the warmest, but I have a few polyester coats that I rotate in.

Because this is your outer layer, it becomes more of your “outfit” as the winter goes on, so make sure to get something you like and that fits your style.
